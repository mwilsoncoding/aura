# Decidability of Recursive Structural Record and Map Subtyping

## Finding

There is a direct theorem for the core combination in issue 35: Castagna and Peyrot prove a sound, complete, terminating procedure for semantic subtyping of regular, contractive recursive types with structural record fields, required and optional fields, closed and open rows, row variables, union, intersection, and negation. This is a result about their precisely defined record calculus, not a general theorem for every language called “structural maps.” In particular, labels are drawn from a countable set of fixed labels; the system excludes first-class/computed labels, recursive row equations, nonregular type unfoldings, and unguarded/noncontractive recursion. The paper gives a relative complexity statement but no explicit complexity class or asymptotic bound. [Castagna and Peyrot, *Polymorphic Records for Dynamic Languages*, PACMPL OOPSLA 2025, DOI 10.1145/3720497](https://doi.org/10.1145/3720497), [version 2 full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.00338v2).

Aura's README names Elixir-like set-theoretic types as a goal but does not specify a formal type grammar. Therefore the theorem establishes decidability for Aura only if its eventual grammar and semantics elaborate into the paper's restricted calculus (or a separately proved extension); this report does not infer such a match from the influence statement. [Aura README](https://github.com/mwilsoncoding/aura/blob/main/README.md).

## Exact Formal System

The paper defines a countable label set `L` and these grammars (renaming its metavariables only for readability):

```text
Kinds       k ::= * | *_bot | Row(L)
Types       t ::= alpha | b | t -> t | { l1=tau1, ..., ln=taun | tail }
                  | t or t | not t | 0
Field types tau ::= theta | t | bot | tau or tau | not tau
Tails       tail ::= rho | epsilon | ..
Rows        r ::= < l1=tau1, ..., ln=taun | tail >_L
                  | r or r | not r
```

Intersections are derived as `A and B = not (not A or not B)`; set difference is `A and not B`. Types are generated coinductively and must be **regular** (finitely many distinct subterms) and **contractive** (each infinite branch passes through infinitely many record or arrow constructors). Field types and rows are generated inductively, so their expressions are finite, although a field type can contain a recursive type. This supports recursive records such as an optional recursive `next` field when the resulting type is regular and contractive; it does not mean row variables themselves may be recursively defined. The grammar and restrictions are in Definition 2.1. [Paper, §2.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.00338v2#S2.SS1).

The record atom has finitely many distinct explicit labels. A field type `t` requires that field to be present with a value in `t`; `t or bot` makes it optional; `bot` denotes absence. `bot` is separate from the empty value type `0`. A closed tail `epsilon` forces every unspecified label absent. An open tail `..` leaves other labels unrestricted, meaning each has field type `1 or bot` (present with any value or absent). A row-variable tail `rho` describes fields over its fixed definition space; row variables are interpreted as sets of partial rows. The formal semantics treats record values as total label-to-value functions with finite support, and rows as partial functions on cofinite label domains. [Paper, §2.1–2.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.00338v2#S2.SS1).

Subtyping is semantic containment of interpretations. The paper reduces `A <: B` to emptiness of `A and not B`, puts Boolean types into a DNF grouped by constructor, and supplies a dedicated emptiness procedure for intersections of polymorphic record atoms. Lemma 2.9 proves that the core record procedure is sound and complete; Proposition 2.10 proves it terminates and concludes that subtyping is decidable. Thus the proof covers recursive record types and all three Boolean connectives in one formal system, not by combining separate results that omit one another's features. [Paper, Definitions 2.6–2.7 and §§2.3, Lemma 2.9, Proposition 2.10](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.00338v2#S2.SS3).

## Complexity and Nearby Results

The authors call their record algorithm backtrack-free and state that its order of complexity is the same as the monomorphic record-subtyping algorithm they identify as used in Elixir. They do **not** state a named complexity class, a parameterized big-O bound, or a lower bound for the recursive row-polymorphic problem. “Same order” is a relative comparison, not an EXPTIME/PSPACE/polynomial classification. The paper's separate tallying/type-inference result must not be substituted for this theorem: tallying is sound but incomplete in the record setting, and the authors list typing as undecidable for their intersection-type system. [Paper, §1.4 and §2.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.00338v2#S1.SS4).

Castagna's earlier *Typing Records, Maps, and Structs* defines a semantic type theory for first-class map-style key sets as well as struct-style labels, with union/intersection and a correct backtrack-free subtyping algorithm. It is relevant evidence for arbitrary/computed map keys, but it is not the missing row-polymorphism theorem: Castagna and Peyrot explicitly describe that earlier setting as lacking row polymorphism. Its result therefore cannot, on its own, establish the combined grammar above; nor does the cited abstract establish recursive row-polymorphic types with complement. [Castagna, PACMPL ICFP 2023, DOI 10.1145/3607838](https://doi.org/10.1145/3607838); [Castagna and Peyrot, §5](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.00338v2#S5).

That distinction also bounds what is not established for Aura by the 2025 proof: arbitrary runtime keys/first-class labels, rows whose variable definitions are recursive, nonregular or noncontractive recursive aliases, or a larger record/map value model than finite-support records over a fixed label universe. The paper explicitly leaves first-class labels out of its calculus and notes that its row-polymorphic extension does not yet include that feature. The abstract theorem also does not prove that Aura's declaration syntax, alias elaboration, or any particular implementation computes the same semantics. [Paper, §1.4 and §5](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.00338v2#S1.SS4).

## Implementation Evidence (Not Proof)

- **CDuce, pinned upstream revision `552553bac0b60717531e36928f73551d3dc7feae` (2026-07-07):** its public type API documents regularity/contractivity, recursive type nodes, union/intersection/difference/negation, and emptiness/subtyping. It represents a record as an openness Boolean plus a finite label map; optionality is represented with a distinguished absent component. This is first-party evidence for the monomorphic recursive record fragment, not row-variable support or a complexity theorem. The 2025 paper says its row-polymorphic extension was a future CDuce implementation goal; the inspected current API still has no row-tail variable in the record representation. [CDuce `types.mli` at pinned revision](https://gitlab.math.univ-paris-diderot.fr/cduce/cduce/-/blob/552553bac0b60717531e36928f73551d3dc7feae/types/types.mli#L4-15), [recursive API](https://gitlab.math.univ-paris-diderot.fr/cduce/cduce/-/blob/552553bac0b60717531e36928f73551d3dc7feae/types/types.mli#L25-46), [record API](https://gitlab.math.univ-paris-diderot.fr/cduce/cduce/-/blob/552553bac0b60717531e36928f73551d3dc7feae/types/types.mli#L234-304), [record emptiness/subtyping implementation](https://gitlab.math.univ-paris-diderot.fr/cduce/cduce/-/blob/552553bac0b60717531e36928f73551d3dc7feae/types/types.ml#L1040-1105), [upstream README](https://gitlab.math.univ-paris-diderot.fr/cduce/cduce/-/blob/552553bac0b60717531e36928f73551d3dc7feae/README.md).
- **Elixir, pinned `main` commit `648b2a94934664cfd2c788348d02d799c68faa69` (2026-09-27):** the first-party descriptor implements recursive type nodes; its structural maps store key domains and fields, distinguish open/closed maps, track a required/optional marker, and implement map-specific union/intersection/difference as part of emptiness-based subtyping. Tests exercise required and optional keys, open/closed maps, and recursive list/tree descriptors. This is significant implementation evidence that structural map shapes, Boolean operations, and recursive descriptors coexist in one checker. It is not a theorem for the paper's row-polymorphic calculus: these internal recursive descriptors are not evidence of user-declared recursively row-polymorphic aliases, and the tests inspected exercise recursive lists/tuples rather than recursive structural maps. [Elixir `Descr` recursive constructor](https://github.com/elixir-lang/elixir/blob/648b2a94934664cfd2c788348d02d799c68faa69/lib/elixir/lib/module/types/descr.ex#L137-L151), [map operations and subtype/emptiness](https://github.com/elixir-lang/elixir/blob/648b2a94934664cfd2c788348d02d799c68faa69/lib/elixir/lib/module/types/descr.ex#L422-L498), [map descriptor representation](https://github.com/elixir-lang/elixir/blob/648b2a94934664cfd2c788348d02d799c68faa69/lib/elixir/lib/module/types/descr.ex#L2814-L2917), [map tests](https://github.com/elixir-lang/elixir/blob/648b2a94934664cfd2c788348d02d799c68faa69/lib/elixir/test/elixir/module/types/map_test.exs#L275-L297), [recursive tests](https://github.com/elixir-lang/elixir/blob/648b2a94934664cfd2c788348d02d799c68faa69/lib/elixir/test/elixir/module/types/recursive_test.exs#L16-L44).

## Bottom Line for Issue 35

**Established:** semantic subtyping is decidable for the exact regular/contractive recursive record grammar of Castagna–Peyrot 2025, including required/optional fields, open/closed tails, row variables, union, intersection, and complement.

**Not established by that theorem:** the same result for unrestricted structural maps with first-class keys, recursive row equations, unguarded/nonregular aliases, or Aura's as-yet-unspecified full grammar. The paper supplies no explicit complexity-class bound. CDuce and Elixir provide implementation evidence for adjacent fragments, not replacements for those missing proof obligations.
